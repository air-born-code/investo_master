# Lens: Situational Awareness — Leopold Aschenbrenner

## Who and what

Leopold Aschenbrenner, a former OpenAI researcher on the superalignment team, published *Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead* in June 2024 — a 165-page argument that AGI arrives around 2027 by straightforward extrapolation of three trend lines: compute (~0.5 orders of magnitude per year), algorithmic efficiency (~0.5 OOM/year), and "unhobbling" gains as models move from chatbot to agent.

He then launched **Situational Awareness LP** to trade the thesis. Reported figures, for context only: roughly $225M of disclosed US equity exposure in September 2024, $1.5B AUM by mid-2025, and above $20B by mid-2026, across roughly 29 holdings. Backers reported to include Patrick and John Collison, Nat Friedman, and Daniel Gross.

What makes the fund interesting is not the return. It is **which** securities express the thesis:

- No Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, Google, or Meta — none of the obvious AI winners.
- Largest disclosed position a **fuel-cell company** (Bloom Energy).
- A 13D activist stake in **Core Scientific**; bitcoin miners held as a proxy for power demand rather than as a crypto position.
- Intel and Broadcom held **while simultaneously short the broader semiconductor sector**, including reported notional put exposure against a semiconductor ETF and against Nvidia.
- Roughly a fifth of assets in Anthropic, a private holding.

The stated organising idea is the **"bottlenecks of the buildout"**: own what constrains AGI infrastructure deployment, not what captures value from AI applications.

Sources are listed at the end. Treat the position detail as reported, not verified — 13F filings disclose long US equity only, and disclose it with a lag.

## Transferable lenses

Ask these of the store. Each is phrased to be answerable from evidence rather than opinion.

### 1. Take the trend line literally, then ask what it requires

The method is extrapolation, not prediction: if a measured rate continues for three more years, what physically has to exist? Compute at ~0.5 OOM/year implies power, land, transformers, cooling and fabrication capacity that must be built by someone.

> For each tracked theme, state the quantitative trend it rests on, its measured rate, and what that rate requires to be physically true in three years. Name what must be built, and by whom.

### 2. Own the binding constraint, not the famous beneficiary

The most-discussed company is rarely the scarcest input. When the constraint moves — from chips to power, from power to interconnect queues — the economics accrue to whoever owns the new bottleneck, and that owner is often in an unglamorous industry.

> For each theme, identify what is scarcest at the margin today, and what would become scarcest if the current constraint were relieved. Which tracked assets own the constraint rather than benefit from it?

### 3. A correct thesis and an overpriced expression are separable

Long the infrastructure and short the semiconductors is not a hedge against being wrong about AI. It is a statement that the thesis is right *and* the popular way to express it is priced for more than it will deliver.

> Where a theme is judged strengthening, state separately whether each linked asset's price already reflects it. Record "theme correct, expression unattractive" as a distinct and legitimate conclusion.

This lens speaks directly to the store's current state: five candidates score 78/78/73/72/72 on quality while all but one score 1/10 on valuation asymmetry. That combination *is* this conclusion, and it deserves to be stated as a finding rather than a stalemate.

### 4. Physical constraints bind before financial ones

Capital was never the limit on the buildout; interconnect queues, turbine lead times, transformer supply and fab capacity are. Physical bottlenecks have long lead times, which makes them both more predictable and harder to compete away.

> For each constraint, record the lead time to relieve it and who holds capacity today. A constraint relievable in under two years is a margin event; one requiring five is a structural position.

### 5. Look for second-order proxies

Bitcoin miners as a power-demand proxy is the clearest example: an asset in one industry whose economics are governed by a constraint in another. These are systematically mispriced because they are screened under the wrong sector.

> Which assets outside a theme's obvious industry have economics governed by that theme's constraint? Name the mechanism linking them, not the correlation.

### 6. A written-off asset can be re-rated by a new demand source

Owning Intel while shorting semiconductors is coherent if the new demand source changes that specific company's economics in a way the sector's rating does not reflect.

> Which assets are priced on a declining legacy business while holding capacity a tracked theme requires? State what the market is pricing and what the theme would need from it.

## What does not transfer

Stated explicitly, because a lens applied outside its conditions does damage.

| Their mechanic | Why it does not transfer |
|---|---|
| Put options and sector shorts | This system performs research and executes nothing. Shorting also imposes a timing requirement that a 5–10 year horizon cannot meet. |
| ~20% in a private holding (Anthropic) | Not accessible, not priceable from public filings, and not analysable under a policy built on primary disclosure. |
| Concentration across ~29 positions | Their position sizing follows from a fund's liquidity, mandate and capital base. Sizing is a separate assessment here, and gate 13 already says so. |
| Activist stakes (13D) | Requires capital and intent this project does not have. |
| The return record | 270% in five months says nothing about a decade-long horizon, and the core prompt's anti-bias rules explicitly warn against reasoning backwards from outcomes. Compelling returns over an 18-month window are consistent with both skill and a single correct macro call. |

## Falsification

The thesis is **unfalsified, not proven**. It is a dated prediction with a specific horizon, which makes it testable — and it must be tested rather than assumed.

What would show it is wrong:

- **Compute scaling decelerates.** The measured rate falls materially below ~0.5 OOM/year for more than a year without an offsetting algorithmic gain.
- **The 2027 window passes without the capability jump.** The essay's central claim is dated. If 2027 arrives and agentic capability has not moved as described, the extrapolation was wrong regardless of what the infrastructure did.
- **The constraint stops being physical.** If interconnect queues shorten, turbine and transformer lead times normalise, and power ceases to be the binding input, the bottleneck premium compresses and the infrastructure position loses its rationale.
- **Demand proves reflexive.** Much of the buildout is funded by firms whose revenues depend on the same expectation. A capex retrenchment would remove the demand that justified the constraint, and the constraint owners are the most exposed to that, not the least.
- **The expression was already priced.** Distinct from the thesis failing: infrastructure assets could deliver everything expected and still return little from a starting valuation that assumed it.

The last two are the ones this system is best placed to test, because both are visible in filings and prices rather than in AI capability claims.
