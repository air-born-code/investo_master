## 1. Executive Summary

The quarter's primary filings landed, and they resolved the Age of Electricity question in an unusual direction: the demand evidence got harder, and so did the evidence against the version of the story the market tells. GE Vernova disclosed a unit path rather than a narrative — gas turbine output planned at 20 GW annualised in Q3 2026, 24 GW in 2028, 30 GW in 2030 against 116 GW contracted or reserved (src-2026-w32-gev-gas-capacity) — while the EIA's own outlook puts US electricity demand growth at 0.9–1.6% a year through 2050, *below* the 2.1% of the past five years (src-2026-w32-eia-aeo-2026). Both are high-reliability primary sources. They are not reconcilable at the national level, and the store now holds the contradiction explicitly rather than only the bullish half.

- **Most important change:** hyperscaler capital expenditure moved from press aggregate to filed fact. Q1 2026 combined capex across the four was $129.8B against $71.9B (src-2026-w32-hyperscaler-capex-q1) — but that run-rate annualises to $519B, materially below the ~$725B full-year figure the theme narrative leans on (src-2026-w32-hyperscaler-runrate). Amazon's Q2 stepped up 23% sequentially (src-2026-w32-amzn-capex-q2); the other three have not filed. The single most load-bearing input to every thesis in this store is a forward commitment, not an established rate.
- **Thesis upgrade:** GE Vernova. Orders +88% organic, backlog $176B, guidance raised (src-2026-w32-gev-q2), and price down 6.4% since W29 (weekly_metrics).
- **Thesis downgrade:** Vertiv — not on results, which were strong, but on disclosure. The Q2 release published no orders, no backlog and no book-to-bill (src-2026-w32-vrt-no-orders). That was the exact metric named as W29's open question (tu-2026-w29-vrt-baseline).
- **Biggest emerging risk:** FERC rejected the amended Susquehanna interconnection agreement 2-1, pushing the arrangement front-of-the-meter (growth_estimates.csv: tln). Co-location structure, not electricity demand, is now the binding variable for merchant nuclear.
- **What deserves attention:** CRDO rose 21.3% and RKLB 22.5% since W29 with no new operating evidence entering the store. MP fell 11.2% with its Q2 10-Q filed and unread.

## 2. Cycle Position

The labour side of the regime board turned, and the two series disagree about which way. The stored caveat for PAYEMS is doing real work here: a single print is close to meaningless and frequently revised by more than the original surprise, and the three-month average is what carries information. The stored caveat for UNRATE is equally load-bearing — the rate can fall for a good reason or a bad one, and the level alone cannot tell you which. I am not treating this month as a turn.

What matters more for this store is that the easing has stalled: core has stopped falling and the policy rate has stopped declining. Watchlist multiples on capital-intensive, project-financed capacity implicitly assume further cuts. That assumption weakened this period without a single order book changing.

On where the buildout sits in its own cycle, the evidence points to *late in demand recognition, early in delivery*:

- PJM's 2027/2028 base residual auction cleared at the FERC-approved cap of $333.44/MW-day and still fell 6,623 MW short of the reliability requirement (src-2026-w32-pjm-capacity). A market that clears at its administrative ceiling and remains short is not signalling a demand cycle; it is signalling a physical one.
- Backlogs are growing at multiples of revenue across the chain — Eaton's electrical backlog +43% against 14% organic growth (src-2026-w32-etn-q2), GE Vernova's Electrification equipment backlog $40.6B, +69% (src-2026-w32-gev-dc-orders). Constraint is capacity, not orders.

Counter-cyclical positioning inside the tracked universe is thin but real. Regulated rate base (NEE, AEP) earns an allowed return on invested capital irrespective of realised load, with political tolerance for bills as the ceiling — the $2.25B of Dominion customer bill credits prices that ceiling explicitly (src-2026-w32-nee-d-merger). Merchant generation is the opposite: VST's economics are directionally exposed to power prices both ways, and the same scarcity lifting them reverses if load forecasts prove high (growth_estimates.csv: vst).

Where the present regime is assumed permanent: in the treatment of a 43% backlog build and a 41% order rate as demand rather than as customers reserving delivery slots. Eaton's own falsifier is the tell — order growth turning negative while backlog stays flat, the signature of cancelled duplicate orders (growth_estimates.csv: etn). Double-ordering is invisible until it unwinds.

## 11. Action posture

**No action.**

Three independent reasons, none of which is discomfort with waiting. First, the gate ledger is empty: not one of the thirteen decision gates is documented for any candidate, so no asset can be proposed for decision review regardless of what the price does. Second, the two names whose theses strengthened this week trade at roughly 21–23× (GEV) and 40–44× (VRT) guided cash flow, and the store holds no scenario inputs with which to test whether either is asymmetric. Third, the two names that rose more than 20% produced no new evidence at all — the correct response to price moving without facts is to do nothing, and to notice that the thing which improved was other people's opinion.

The most valuable outcome of this week is not GE Vernova's order book. It is that the EIA's demand forecast and the hyperscaler capex run-rate now sit in the store as high-reliability contradictions, next to the bull case, dated. A theme that survives its own disconfirming evidence is worth more than one that has never met any.
